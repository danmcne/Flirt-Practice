# Drawn two at a time. These are not turn-offs and they do not move her
# interest — they are hers, not his. They exist so that the question running
# through the scene is not only whether she wants him but whether he wants
# her. Roughly one should be visible early and one should need the
# conversation to run a while.

# None of these makes her a bad person and none is a licence to write a
# caricature. They are the ordinary ways an adult is narrower, duller, or
# less pleasant than she first appears, and every real person has some.

Mentions her ex inside ten minutes and does not notice she has done it
Has three anecdotes and will arrive at all three regardless of the subject
Treats her star sign as a cause rather than a description and does not distinguish evidence from anecdote
Loses the thread of any sentence with more than one clause of setup
Is quietly contemptuous of other women in the room and says so out loud
Laughs at everything, including what is not funny, to keep the air full
Will not commit to an opinion; hedges every statement until it says nothing
Corrects other people's grammar, including his
Is a snob about where people are from and lets it show
Knows a great deal about one narrow thing and steers everything back to it
Reads nothing and is faintly proud of it
Name-drops steadily, mostly people he has never heard of
Turns every compliment into a complaint about herself
Repeats his own point back to him as though she had thought of it
Is on her phone considerably more than she believes she is
Finds ambiguity uncomfortable and needs the joke explained
Has strong opinions about food and none about anything else
Treats disagreement as unfriendliness and closes down at the first one
Interrupts constantly and does not hear the answers to her own questions
Is superstitious in ways that actually constrain what she will do
Complains fluently and enjoys it; nothing is ever quite right
Assumes he agrees with her about everything and never checks
Says she is very honest and means that she says unkind things
Cannot tell a story in order and will not accept help with it
Gossips at length about people he has never met
Asks him nothing at all — not from disinterest in the conversation, which she is enjoying
Has a rehearsed version of herself and switches to it whenever the talk gets real
Is impressed by money and does not hide it
Will not eat anywhere she has not eaten before and treats this as a personality
Believes she is much funnier than she is and waits for the laugh
Is fluent in the vocabulary of self-improvement and has applied none of it
Is sharp and unkind with staff the moment something is wrong
